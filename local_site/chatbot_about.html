About the PLATO Chatbot

The PLATO Chatbot answers questions about ESA's PLATO mission from the papers written about it: the publications collected in PLATO-Pub. It is there to save you time in getting to know the instrument, its data and what has been published about them, and to lead you to the papers where the details are.

What it knows

The chatbot works from the papers on the list of Published PLATO Papers, which PLATO-Pub compiles by automated searches of NASA ADS, and it takes in new papers as they appear on the list. For each question it searches these papers for the passages that best match it, and a large language model writes the answer from those passages.

Where a paper is openly available, because its authors put it online (on arXiv, for instance) or because the publisher released it under an open licence, the chatbot has as a rule read the full text. Of the other papers, many of them conference proceedings behind a paywall, it has read only the abstract, as a public service may not pass on their text. An abstract does not show everything a paper covers, so for these papers follow the link to the paper itself.

You can also ask about the papers themselves: which papers there are on a topic, which ones an author has written (give the surname, and say so if you mean first author only), or what was published in a given year.

How to read its answers

Statements taken from the papers carry numbered citations: [1], [2] and so on. See sources, under the answer, lists the passages cited, with the paper, when it was published and the section the passage comes from, and links to the paper on NASA ADS, at the publisher, and on arXiv where there is a copy.

PLATO's design and schedule have changed over the years (the number of cameras, the field of view, the launch date), so an older paper can be out of date. Where papers disagree, the chatbot goes with the most recent one and says so. The dates in the sources show how old each piece of information is.

When the papers do not cover an astronomy question, the chatbot may answer from the language model's general knowledge instead. It says so first, and puts that part in a paragraph of its own that begins with General knowledge, not from the PLATO publications. Such a paragraph has no citations and has not been checked against the papers; on facts about PLATO itself it may be out of date.

What it cannot do

Like anything built on a language model, it makes mistakes: it can misread a passage, or combine two that do not belong together. Check the cited passages before you rely on a figure, and cite the papers, not the chatbot.

It knows what the published papers say and nothing else: not the consortium's internal documents, and not ESA's own pages, such as those on observing proposals. Its answers are not statements of ESA or the PLATO Mission Consortium. It answers questions about PLATO and astronomy only.

Your conversation

The chatbot reads each question together with the few messages before it, so you can ask follow-up questions. For a new topic, start a New conversation.

The conversation is kept in this browser tab, so that you can visit other pages and come back to it. Closing the tab removes it.

Questions and answers are also logged on the server, so that the answers can be checked and the chatbot improved. The search and the language model run on Aarhus University's servers: your questions are not sent to an outside AI company. Please do not enter personal information.

To be confirmed before the chatbot goes public: what is logged, for how long, who can see it, and whom to contact about it.
